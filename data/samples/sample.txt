Artificial Intelligence is transforming modern technology. It allows machines to perform tasks that traditionally required human intelligence. These tasks include recognising images, understanding speech, translating languages and making decisions. AI systems are becoming increasingly common in healthcare, education, finance and transportation.

Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence. Instead of following explicitly programmed instructions for every task, machine learning models identify patterns in data. They use these patterns to make predictions or decisions. The quality of a machine learning model often depends on the quantity and quality of its training data. Common approaches include supervised learning, unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning.

Natural Language Processing enables computers to understand and work with human language. It is used in chatbots, translation applications, search engines and virtual assistants. Modern NLP systems frequently use transformer architectures. These models can understand relationships between words and their surrounding context. Large language models are trained on enormous collections of text to generate human-like responses.

Retrieval Augmented Generation combines information retrieval with language generation. Rather than relying entirely on information stored during training, a RAG system retrieves relevant documents before generating an answer. Documents are divided into smaller chunks, converted into numerical embeddings and stored in a vector database. When a user submits a question, the system retrieves relevant chunks and provides them as context to a language model.

Chunking is an important part of building a reliable RAG pipeline. If chunks are too large, retrieval may include irrelevant information. If chunks are too small, important context may be lost. Overlapping chunks help preserve information across boundaries. Metadata such as document names, page numbers and paragraph numbers can also be stored alongside each chunk. This makes it possible to identify the original source of retrieved information.
